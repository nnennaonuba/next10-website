    How the check works, and what it cannot tell you

    Contribution before ads, per order. The price paid, less VAT if your sales include it, product cost, TikTok commission, creator commission on the affiliate share, fulfilment, returns, promotions, sampling and other selling costs.

    before ads = price − VAT (if included) − product cost − price × (commission + returns + promotions + sampling + other) − price × creator % × affiliate share − fulfilment

    Contribution after marketing. Before ads, less ad spend per order. Margin is that figure as a share of price. This is the vertical axis on the chart.

    Estimated break-even ROAS. The ROAS at which ad spend uses up everything left before ads. TikTok's reported GMV Max ROI counts gross revenue including some organic and affiliate orders, so a textbook calculation on your sales figure gives a different answer. Where you enter the reported ROAS, we put the break-even on that basis by scaling it by how far your actual spend sits from the spend you can afford:

    break-even ROAS = TikTok ROAS × ad spend ÷ (before ads × orders)

    For a momentum target, the affordable spend rises by the loss you accept on every order. If an offer loses money before any ads, or has nothing left for ads, no ROAS makes it pay back, and the check says so.

    The read. Velocity is orders in the period, margin is contribution after marketing, and materiality is bubble size (sales value). The dividing lines are the two thresholds you set. The four reads are candidates for a decision, not decisions.

    What it cannot tell you. It reads Shop orders only, from past figures. It does not show whether a sale would have happened anyway, what a new customer is worth over time, what moved to your website, Amazon or a retailer, stock position, or your fixed overheads. Those are the questions in Chapters 4 and 5 of the report, and the ones we work on with brands.
